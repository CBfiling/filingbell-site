How it works

Three steps, none of them yours.

1

We read every 8-K, daily

Every new filing on EDGAR is scanned for the exact language companies use when something structural is happening — strategic reviews, special committees, mergers, bankruptcies.

2

We rank by certainty

A signed merger agreement isn't the same as a board "considering its options." We sort by how confirmed the event actually is, not just how recent.

3

It will land in your inbox

Once the digest opens, one email each morning. Every entry links straight to the primary filing on EDGAR — we point, you decide.

Why I built this

One person's research tool, not a company.

I spend a lot of time digging through SEC filings by hand — looking for the kind of mispriced, overlooked situations that don't show up on a normal stock screener. A board quietly hiring a bank to "explore alternatives," a special committee getting formed, a company trading at a steep discount to book value right as something changes.

The problem is that information is buried in individual 8-K filings, not a searchable database, and by the time it shows up in a headline or a stock screener, it's often old news. I got tired of manually checking EDGAR every day, so I built a scraper that does it for me. Measuring what happens after these filings is ongoing work — the first backtest had a flaw and has been withdrawn while a corrected version is built.

This is a solo project, still early. I use it myself every morning, and I'm sharing it because I figured other people doing similar research might find it useful too.

Questions

The obvious questions, answered.

Is it really free?
The digest isn't open yet — right now there's a waitlist. It will be free during early access, with no paid tier and no credit card. If that changes later, subscribers will hear about it first — nothing gets charged without asking.
What do you do with my email?
For now it's used only to tell you when the daily digest opens, and nothing else. It isn't sold or shared for marketing. It's collected by Formspree, the service that runs the waitlist form, which stores it and forwards each signup to my email inbox. When the digest opens, your address will be moved to an email delivery service, and you'll be asked to confirm before receiving the digest — if you don't confirm, you won't get it. Every digest will include an unsubscribe link. To be removed from the waitlist before then, send a note through the suggestion form below and include the email address you signed up with.
Is this investment advice?
No. FilingBell surfaces public SEC filings and points you to the primary source. It never tells you to buy or sell anything — you read the actual filing and decide for yourself.
How is this different from a stock screener?
Standard screeners filter on numbers (P/E, market cap, ROE). They have no concept of "this company just told the SEC it's exploring a sale," because that lives in unstructured filing text, not a data field. FilingBell reads that text.
Where did the backtest numbers go?
They were withdrawn after an audit found that the outcome classification counted many companies as “acquired” that were still independently listed. Rather than leave inaccurate figures up, they've been removed while a corrected, clearly hypothetical backtest is built. Details are on the track record page.
